ResoNote, three ways

Three parallel designs of the same easy scenario: Clarence learns the chorus of a new piece, records it, gets one thing to fix from the AI coach, and drills it.

Each design has the same 10 screens so we can compare them side by side. Screens are static UI only. The song "Northern Lights" and all scores are placeholder data, not real user results.

A

Score-first

The sheet music is the workspace. Loops and mistakes are drawn on the score like a highlighter and a red pencil.

View 10 screens
B

Timeline-first

No score. The song is a colour-coded waveform of sections with big lyrics, made for singers who can't read music.

View 10 screens
C

Coach-first

The AI coach runs the session as a chat and brings in the player, recorder and tuner as cards when needed.

View 10 screens

How they differ

A. Score-firstB. Timeline-firstC. Coach-first
Choosing a loopTap bars on the score, drag the highlightDrag two handles on the waveformTap a phrase the coach suggests
Seeing mistakesRed circles on the notes, with cents offColoured strip under the lyrics, high-low pitch lineCoach describes it, you compare audio clips
Picking your partBy staff and note rangeBy listening to samplesPick, listen, or hum and let the coach match
Drill styleChecklist of steps with a pitch graphCall and response (echo)Coach-guided steps, says when it adapts
Best forSingers who read a little and want to learnSingers who can't read a score at allBusy singers who want to be told what to do
Main riskScore still intimidating for non-readersHard to talk to the director in bar numbersSlower for users who know what they want
